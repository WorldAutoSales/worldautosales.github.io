Powertrain Mix by Country

New car registrations by powertrain (Battery Electric, Plug-in Hybrid, Hybrid, Petrol, Diesel, Others) across Europe, the Americas, Asia, and Africa. Countries ranked by BEV (battery-electric) share. Use the filters to zoom into a powertrain, country, or period.

  • Norway leads the world in EV adoption by a wide margin — battery-electric alone made up over 97% of May 2026 registrations, a market so far along the transition that petrol and diesel are now a rounding error.
  • Some of the largest EU markets (Germany, France, Italy) still sit in the middle of the pack once hybrids are counted — hybrid-electric (not battery-electric) is the single most popular powertrain in most of Europe right now.
  • Canada joined the mix starting January 2026. Statistics Canada publishes registrations quarterly (not monthly like Europe's ACEA data), so it appears as its own selectable period rather than being blended with Europe's figures — select "January 2026" to see it.
  • "July 2026" only covers 25 markets (Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Chile, China, Colombia, Ecuador, India, Indonesia, Israel, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, Nepal, New Zealand, Peru, Singapore, South Korea, Taiwan, Thailand, Turkey, Ukraine, United States, Uruguay, Vietnam) — ACEA hasn't published Europe's July figures yet (their calendar skips a standalone July release; the next one, due late September, covers August). The page still defaults to "June 2026" for that reason, since it's the most recent period with Europe included; switch to July manually if you want the smaller, more current set. Ukraine's figure is new passenger cars only — a small fraction of its total market (see the Country Detail table for the caveat).
  • "August 2026" only covers 5 markets so far (India, South Korea, Israel, Albania, Paraguay) — it's shown anyway rather than waiting for broader coverage, so the newest available data isn't hidden. More countries join this period as their August figures are published; the page still defaults to "June 2026" in the meantime.
  • "2026 (Projected)" is an estimate, not a real annual measurement. For every country with a real monthly breakdown — the 31 ACEA countries, New Zealand, Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Chile, China, India, Israel, Japan, Mexico, South Korea, Taiwan, and the United States (44 markets) — each fuel is trended independently: the rate is the higher of (the average of its last 3 actual months) or (its year-to-date average), applied to the remaining months and added onto the actual year-to-date total; the "YTD floor" means a fuel that's cooled off recently is never projected below plain run-rate, while one that's accelerating keeps accelerating rather than flattening out like a simple year-end multiplication would. This is recalculated from scratch every time a new month of actuals arrives. Canada, the Philippines, and Turkey stay on plain run-rate (YTD figure × 12/months): the Philippines has no monthly breakdown in the database at all, Canada has only a single real quarterly data point rather than 3 distinct months to trend from, and Turkey's monthly figures haven't been switched over to the trended method yet. Check the "Data Through" column for which method applies to each row.
  • Select "2026 YTD" to see this year's progress so far — but each country is only summed through its own latest available data (Europe through June, Canada through Q1/March only, Mexico, Japan, Australia, Brazil, Argentina, Turkey, the Philippines, New Zealand, China, and the United States through July, India a partial-year snapshot from its live dashboard with no exact cutoff date). Check the "Data Through" column in the Country Detail table below for each country's exact cutoff before comparing totals across countries.
  • Select "2025 (Annual)" to compare 2025 across all 55 markets tracked here at once — the 31 ACEA countries and Canada alongside India, the United States, China, Mexico, Japan, Brazil, Indonesia, Thailand, Argentina, Turkey, the Philippines, New Zealand, and South Africa, which are only available as annual figures since their official sources don't publish a monthly/quarterly powertrain split. Because it mixes a full year with single-month/quarter periods elsewhere in the list, periods still aren't blended together — pick one period at a time. China's, South Africa's, and Argentina's petrol/diesel splits in particular rely partly on disclosed percentage shares or secondary reporting rather than exact published units and should be read as approximate. Japan's figures cover registered passenger cars only and exclude kei-cars (roughly 35-40% of Japan's new-car market) and commercial vehicles; Australia's (FCAI/VFACTS) cover light vehicles only, excluding heavy trucks and buses, and its 2022 BEV figure understates real sales because Tesla — Australia's best-selling EV brand — didn't report to VFACTS for part of that year; Brazil's dominant Flex-fuel (dual gasoline/ethanol) vehicles are folded into Petrol rather than split out; South Korea's national data doesn't separate plug-in hybrids from regular hybrids, so PHEV is folded into HEV there; Indonesia's and the Philippines' sources don't publish a diesel/petrol split, so all combustion volume is folded into Petrol; Thailand's total includes pickups and other light commercial vehicles (not passenger cars only), and its Petrol/Diesel split is an assumption based on market structure (passenger≈petrol, commercial≈diesel) rather than a fuel-type figure FTI publishes directly; Turkey's figures (like Japan's) cover passenger cars only, excluding light commercial vehicles, and its "Others" bucket is LPG-converted vehicles (Otogaz), a notable category in the Turkish market; New Zealand's figures (like Japan's and Turkey's) cover light passenger vehicles only, excluding commercial vehicles (utes/trucks, a large diesel-heavy segment of NZ's market); the Philippines' 2025 figure covers only Jan-Nov (11 of 12 months) — check "Data Through" for that row; Taiwan's figures cover passenger cars (小客車) only, excluding commercial vehicles, and — like South Korea — don't separate PHEV from HEV; Vietnam's figures merge three non-overlapping sources (VAMA member brands, VinFast, and TC Group/Hyundai) and still exclude smaller independent importers, so likely undercount the true market slightly, and PHEV isn't tracked by any of the three. None of these is directly comparable to other countries' all-vehicle figures. Most of Asia, Africa, South America, and Oceania remain unsourced beyond these fifteen; those countries aren't at zero, they just haven't been added yet.
  • China gained a real monthly breakdown for 2026 (January-July, selectable as individual months, "2026 YTD", and "2026 (Projected)") — source: CAAM (China Association of Automobile Manufacturers)'s own monthly wholesale figures via CnEVPost, i.e. automaker deliveries to dealers including exports, the same basis this page's pre-existing 2021-2025 annual China rows already use (their totals match OICA's whole-market figures exactly). This is a different, more exact source than the percentage-derived annual splits flagged in the "2025 (Annual)" note above — China's 2026 BEV/PHEV figures are CAAM's own exact published units, not derived from disclosed shares. HEV is 0 throughout because China's official "NEV" (New Energy Vehicle) classification covers BEV+PHEV only; non-plug-in hybrids sell in China but are counted within the conventional/ICE segment, so they're folded into Petrol along with the (unpublished, believed small) Diesel share.
  • "2024 (Annual)" and "2023 (Annual)" give the same comparison for earlier years (52 markets each) — a few countries with only a partial fuel-type split for those years (e.g. Argentina, Malaysia) or no matching year at all aren't included, to avoid faking a complete row. Click any country — on the map, or its name in the table below — to open its own page with the full year-by-year trend, including a handful of markets (Malaysia, Colombia, Peru) that couldn't go into any comparison period here but still have a real trend to show.
  • "2022 (Annual)" through "2020 (Annual)" extend the same comparison further back (46/45/40 markets respectively). A handful of country-years in this range had one fuel field genuinely unpublished by the source rather than a real zero (Morocco and Italy 2020 are the largest cases) — rather than drop those countries, the unpublished amount is folded into "Others," so for those specific rows "Others" partly represents an unknown/unpublished category, not just genuine minor fuel types (LPG, CNG, etc.). Every tracked country stays visible in every year as a result.
  • A handful of Eastern European markets (Latvia, Romania, Bulgaria) remain almost entirely petrol/diesel, showing how uneven the EV transition still is within the EU itself.
  • However quickly smaller countries move into EVs, overall Europe's progression depends heavily on the Big 5 (Germany, UK, France, Italy, Spain) — they account for 68.6% of total registrations across the 31 markets tracked here (May 2026), so their pace sets the continent's pace far more than any single fast-moving small market can.
