World Car Sales · Overview

How many light vehicles does the world sell and drive?

Every figure on this page counts all light vehicles — passenger cars, SUVs, pickups and vans (light commercial vehicles) — and excludes heavy trucks and buses. It shows worldwide new-vehicle sales since 2000, the battery-electric (BEV) versus combustion (ICE) split of those sales, and an estimate of how many light vehicles are on the road. World totals only, no country split.

11A · Global light-vehicle sales, per year

Worldwide new light-vehicle sales (passenger cars, SUVs, pickups and light vans), 2000–2025. 2010–2025 are the IEA's measured figures (2025 is an IEA estimate); 2000–2009 are my estimate, drawn dashed with open dots (see Notes). No light-vehicle series exists for earlier years, so the page starts in 2000.

11B · New light-vehicle sales: BEV vs ICE (world)

Worldwide light-duty (passenger car and light commercial) sales by powertrain, 2010–2025, from the IEA Global EV Data Explorer. "ICE & hybrids" is every light vehicle that cannot be plugged in — petrol, diesel, LPG/CNG and conventional (non-plug-in) hybrids. No country split.

11C · Estimated light vehicles on the road (world), since 2000

Modelled worldwide stock of light-duty vehicles (cars and light commercial vehicles) in use, 2000–2025: last year's fleet minus scrapped vehicles plus this year's sales, calibrated to the fleet size the IEA implies for 2010–2024. The electric part (BEV and PHEV) is built from cumulative IEA sales and reconciled to the IEA's measured electric-car stock. This is an estimate, not a census — see Notes for the method and its error.

  • Scope. "Light vehicles" means passenger cars plus light commercial vehicles (SUVs, pickups, vans) — the IEA's "light-duty vehicles" and the same basis as the country totals on the Mix by Country page (the 2024 sum of the site's countries, 84.8 million, is close to the IEA's world figure of 83.3 million). Heavy trucks, buses and two/three-wheelers are excluded everywhere on this page. OICA's narrower "passenger car" series (67.5 million in 2024) is not used because OICA files many SUVs and pickups — most of the US market — under commercial vehicles.
  • 11A sales, 2010–2025. IEA Global EV Data Explorer (Global EV Outlook 2026), read through Our World in Data: electric plus non-electric car sales. 2025 values are IEA estimates (84.8 million).
  • 11A sales, 2000–2009 (estimate). No free worldwide light-vehicle sales series exists for these years. I scaled OICA passenger-car sales (2005–2009) and US Bureau of Transportation Statistics passenger-car production (2000–2004, production used as a sales proxy) by the light-vehicle/passenger-car ratio observed in 2010–2024 (IEA ÷ OICA) extrapolated backwards — 1.07 in 2000 rising to 1.23 in 2024. Treat these ten years as ±5%.
  • Why not earlier than 2000? Earlier worldwide series (Ward's, OICA) only count all motor vehicles or only passenger cars, not light vehicles, and I did not want to invent a heavy-vehicle share. The earlier all-vehicle and passenger-car charts were removed from this page for that reason.
  • 11B. IEA Global EV Data Explorer via Our World in Data: light-vehicle sales (electric + non-electric), electric sales (BEV + PHEV) and the BEV share of electric sales. "ICE & hybrids" is every light vehicle that cannot be plugged in — petrol, diesel, LPG/CNG and conventional (non-plug-in) hybrids; the IEA does not publish a global hybrid (HEV) sales series, so HEV can't be split out. 2025 values are IEA estimates.
  • 11C method. Fleett = Fleett−1 × (1 − st) + Salest, with the scrappage rate s falling linearly from 4.5% (2000) to 3.3% (2025) and the 2000 starting fleet (631 million) fitted — together — by least squares to the total fleet the IEA implies for 2010–2024 (IEA electric stock ÷ IEA electric share of stock). Fit error against those 15 anchor years is within about ±3%. Sales for 2000–2009 are the estimates above, so the early years are the least certain. The IEA-implied totals are shown as dots; the IEA's own 2025 total is not used because its 2025 stock share is rounded to a whole percent (5%).
  • 11C electric part. BEV and PHEV stocks are cumulative IEA sales with a constant 1.5% yearly loss, the value that best reproduces the IEA's measured global electric-car stock (76 million at end-2025 per GEVO 2026; model: 77 million, within ±3% in every year 2014–2025; the very small 2011–2013 stocks differ by up to 10%). BEV/PHEV before 2010 are negligible (~30,000 vehicles at end-2009) and ICE & hybrids is the rest of the fleet, so it includes conventional hybrids. Heavy trucks, buses and two/three-wheelers are not in the fleet — OICA's wider "vehicles in use" count is considerably larger.
  • Rounding: values are shown in millions of vehicles; hover the charts for exact figures.
Sources: IEA Global EV Data Explorer / Global EV Outlook 2026 via Our World in Data · OICA passenger-car sales (2005–2009 estimate input) · US Bureau of Transportation Statistics, National Transportation Statistics Table 1-22 (2000–2004 estimate input) · fleet model: this site's estimate
