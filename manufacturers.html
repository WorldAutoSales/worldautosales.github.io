New car registrations by brand, and how much of each brand's volume is already battery-electric. This is a new, much smaller dimension than the country-level pages — real per-brand powertrain splits are only publicly available for a couple of markets so far, so this page starts narrow and honest rather than approximate and global.

Germany — full Petrol/Diesel/BEV/PHEV/HEV split by brand

Source: Kraftfahrt-Bundesamt (KBA), Germany's federal motor authority — "Neuzulassungen von Personenkraftwagen nach Marken und Modellreihen" (new passenger-car registrations by make and model series). This is the only market in this project with a genuine, complete powertrain split at brand level, so it's the only one where "% of a brand's sales that are BEV" is a real, directly-sourced number rather than an estimate.

Toyota Canada (TCI) — total sales & electrification

Source: Toyota Canada Inc. (TCI) Newsroom press releases (media.toyota.ca) — a real total-market figure, unlike the iZEV chart below. TCI only ever reports Toyota+Lexus combined, never Toyota alone. Before 2023, TCI didn't disclose a Zero-Emission Vehicle (ZEV) figure at all, so "Electrified" (green+blue below) is shown as a single HEV bucket even though a small, growing BEV/PHEV share (RAV4 Prime from 2020-21, bZ4X from 2022) is folded in. 2023-2025 separate a combined ZEV (BEV+PHEV+FCEV, shown as the "BEV" segment) from HEV, but still don't split BEV from PHEV. Only 2026 (Jan-Jun, from the Q1/Q2 releases) has genuine separate BEV and PHEV figures.

Canada — EV registrations by brand

Source: Transport Canada's iZEV incentive program (open.canada.ca) — this counts rebate claims for battery-electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles, not total registrations. Canada doesn't publish a free per-brand Petrol/Diesel/HEV breakdown, so there's no total-market denominator here and no "% of sales" figure — this chart ranks brands purely by BEV+PHEV volume, a different kind of measurement than the Germany and Toyota TCI charts above. Toyota/Lexus are excluded here since Toyota Canada's own real sales figures (above) replace this claims-based estimate for that brand.

BEV by brand — confirmed floor, not exhaustive

These 4 markets (Netherlands, Italy, Ireland, Czechia) publish real per-model unit counts by national registration authority, but no fuel-type column — so BEV here is a conservative estimate: a brand's units only count as BEV when the model NAME itself is unambiguous (e.g. Tesla, VW ID.*, Skoda Enyaq/Elroq, Kia EV3/6/9, BMW/Mini i-prefix). Models sold under the same name in both a combustion/hybrid AND a battery-electric version (e.g. Hyundai Kona, Kia Niro, Volvo XC40) can't be split this way and are left in "Unclassified" rather than guessed — so the true per-brand BEV share is higher than shown here, sometimes substantially. Cross-checked against each country's real official BEV total (ACEA) as a ceiling: this method typically captures 65-82% of it in the Netherlands/Ireland/Czechia, but only 14-32% in Italy for 2023-2025 (its source groups powertrains much more coarsely) rising to 67% by 2026.
