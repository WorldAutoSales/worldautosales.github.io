Yearly trend

  • This page tracks total market size — every powertrain combined — not the BEV/PHEV/HEV/Petrol/Diesel split. It's the broadest, longest-running dataset on the site (147 countries vs. 32 on the powertrain pages) precisely because a plain total is far easier to source globally than a powertrain breakdown.
  • Coverage isn't uniform: most major markets run through 2025 (2026 partial), but roughly 80 smaller countries stop at 2018 because their public source stopped reporting country-level totals after that year — those markets aren't zero, they're just not updated past 2018.
  • Precision also varies by source. Major markets (Europe, North America, most of Asia) come from official national or regional statistics agencies. A handful of countries were reconstructed from secondary press coverage rather than an official table — treat single-country outliers with some caution rather than as exact figures.
  • 2026 is a partial year in progress. Wherever a market has enough 2026 data to extrapolate, this page shows a "2026 Proj." estimate instead of the raw partial total — everywhere: the stat row, the Market Size Trend chart, the Total Registrations bars (country name in accent color), the Market Size Map (hover for the note), the country drilldown chart, and the Momentum sparklines ("Proj." tag) — not an official forecast. It currently covers 45 markets. For most of them it's a simple rule of three (actual registrations so far this year, divided by months of actual data, times 12); Canada's is the least reliable of these, extrapolated from a single StatCan quarter. For the 7 markets with a real month-by-month breakdown (Argentina, Brazil, China, India, Japan, Mexico, United States), each fuel's own volume is instead trended from the higher of its last-3-months average or its year-to-date average, so an accelerating market keeps accelerating rather than flattening out. Markets without enough 2026 data still show their latest raw actual year.
Sources vary by country — national/regional statistics agencies (OICA-style aggregation), Statistics Canada, Argonne National Laboratory, and others; see the notes above.
