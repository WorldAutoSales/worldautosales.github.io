Then & Now — the Motorisation Explorer

Every country, three eras: 2010, 2020 and the latest data on file. X-axis is population (log scale), Y-axis is cars per 100 people (or litres of oil per person) for whichever metric tab is selected above — always on a per-person basis — bubble size is the total (cars sold / cars on the road / litres of oil). Press Play to watch markets move — bubbles that race upward are climbing fastest on whichever metric is selected (more cars sold, more cars on the road, or more oil burned per person); bubbles drifting right are simply growing their population. Only countries with data in all three eras can animate; everyone else is left out of this one chart only.

  • Population: World Bank (SP.POP.TOTL) for 105 countries — "Latest" uses 2024, the 2010/2020 snapshots use the matching historical year from the same series; Taiwan from its own Ministry of Interior (household-registration population, year-end 2010 / 2020 / 2024); France's five overseas departments from INSEE/IEDOM (2025 for "Latest"; 2010/2020 use each department's nearest official census year — Mayotte's 2010 figure is actually its 2007 census, the closest one on file, since Mayotte didn't join the standard population survey until 2017). All 110 countries have a population figure at every one of the three snapshot years.
  • Sales per capita: "Latest" uses each country's latest full-year total from the "Auto Markets by Country" page — a 2026 full-year run-rate projection where available (flagged "proj." in the tooltip), otherwise the latest complete raw year. The 2010/2020 snapshots use that exact calendar year from the same dataset — 103 of 110 countries have at least one of the two, 73 have both. The remaining gaps (mostly smaller markets whose own tracked series stops around 2018, plus Laos, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Rwanda and Suriname, which have little or no sales history on this site at all) would need new country-by-country research to close — OICA's consolidated sales-by-country archive could fill most of them at once, but it sits behind a commercial-use pricing agreement this site hasn't accepted.
  • Adult population (15+) and working-age population (15-64): only available for "Latest" (2024 age-split shares from World Bank / UN World Population Prospects, Taiwan from its Ministry of the Interior) — there's no historical age-split loaded yet, so the per-15-64 and per-adult tabs disable themselves on the 2010/2020 snapshots.
  • Cars on the road per capita: "Latest" uses each country's 2024 (or nearest earlier year) fleet size from this site's cars-on-road dataset (50 of 110 countries); the 2010/2020 snapshots use that model's own value for those exact years, same 50 countries — real registry counts for most major markets, modeled/anchored estimates for the rest.
  • Oil consumption per capita: total refined petroleum product consumption (not just road fuel), from the Energy Institute Statistical Review of World Energy / EIA via Our World in Data — covers 105 countries at "Latest" (2024) and at both 2010 and 2020. Converted from terawatt-hours to litres using a blended ~36 MJ/L energy density (an approximation). This is not split into petrol vs. diesel — no source publishes that split per country in one consistent place.
  • Then & Now bubble chart: only plots countries that have population, the selected metric's total, and continent data for 2010, 2020 and latest simultaneously — fewer countries than the map/rank list above, since every one of the three eras has to be present. A country vanishing from one era to the next in this chart means it dropped a data point, not that its market disappeared.
  • Different vintages are being compared against one population year at "Latest" — sales figures run up to a 2026 projection, fleet and oil figures are 2024. Treat this as a reasonable approximation, not a perfectly synchronized snapshot.
Population: World Bank, Taiwan MOI, INSEE/IEDOM. Sales: this site's own Auto Markets by Country dataset. Fleet: this site's own Cars on the Road dataset. Oil consumption: Energy Institute / EIA via Our World in Data (CC BY 4.0).
