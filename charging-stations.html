Who runs the chargers: brands and speeds

  • Points per country (IEA). Public charging points from the IEA Global EV Data Explorer (Global EV Outlook 2026 data set, CC BY 4.0): slow up to 22 kW, fast above 22 kW up to 150 kW, ultra-fast above 150 kW. The IEA rounds to two or three significant digits and covers about 50 countries; the rest only appear through Open Charge Map. "Fast + ultra-fast" adds the two upper classes (everything above 22 kW).
  • Point, port, connector, station. A charging point (IEA, Europe) and a port (US DOE, Open Charge Map) are the same thing: one socket position that charges one vehicle at a time. A connector is the plug itself; one point can offer several (CCS and CHAdeMO, for instance) but still serves a single car, so counting connectors would inflate the total. A station is the location and hosts one or several points. When Open Charge Map gives no number of points for a station, the ports are counted as the sum of its connectors, which can overstate them slightly. The IEA publishes points only; stations come from Open Charge Map and, where an official count exists, from the US DOE (83,559 stations, 262,578 ports) and Natural Resources Canada (14,743 locations, 39,654 public ports, March 2026).
  • Open Charge Map is a sample. It is a crowd-sourced registry that also imports operator and government feeds, so its coverage varies a lot: close to complete in the United States, Canada, Norway, Spain, Iceland and New Zealand; partial in Germany, France, the United Kingdom or Italy; nearly empty in China, South Korea and Vietnam. The "OCM coverage" column divides OCM ports by the IEA total; read the brand and speed splits as the make-up of the mapped network, not of the whole country. Private and removed chargers are excluded; ports = the listed number of points, else the sum of connectors.
  • Speed bands come from the power of each connector in Open Charge Map (<50, 50–99, 100–249, 250–349, 350+ kW); ports of a station are spread over its connectors in proportion. Missing power counts as under 50 kW.
  • Tesla Superchargers come from supercharge.info (community database, open sites only); Tesla also appears in Open Charge Map under several operator names, so use the Tesla box for Tesla and ignore the Tesla line of the OCM list when they differ.
  • Ratios. Points per 100,000 people aged 15–64 use the working-age population of this site's Population & Energy page (latest year, applied to every year). Points per 1,000 EVs divide the IEA points by the IEA EV stock (battery-electric + plug-in hybrid cars, 2024 and 2025 only).
  • China: the IEA counts 4.7 million public points (more than the rest of the world together) but Open Charge Map lists almost none. The brand card therefore uses the China Charging Alliance (EVCIPA) ranking of public charging piles by operator for July 2025 (top 15 = 84.1% of public piles), without station or speed split; the Tesla box is available.
Charging points: IEA Global EV Data Explorer (CC BY 4.0). Stations, brands, power, density: Open Charge Map (open registry; data providers' licences apply), US DOE Alternative Fuels Data Center, supercharge.info. Population: World Bank / UN. Map boundaries: Natural Earth 1:10m via world-atlas (public domain).
