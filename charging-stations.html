Who runs the chargers: companies and speeds

  • Ports per country (IEA). Public charging ports (the IEA calls them charging points) from the IEA Global EV Data Explorer (Global EV Outlook 2026 data set, CC BY 4.0): slow up to 22 kW, fast above 22 kW up to 150 kW, ultra-fast above 150 kW. The IEA (which calls them charging points) rounds to two or three significant digits and covers about 50 countries; the rest only appear through Open Charge Map. "Fast + ultra-fast" adds the two upper classes (everything above 22 kW).
  • Port, connector, station. This page uses one word, port: one socket position that charges one car at a time. It is what the IEA and the EU call a charging point and what the US DOE and Open Charge Map call a port or point. A connector is the plug itself; one port can offer several (CCS and CHAdeMO, for instance) but still serves a single car, so counting connectors would inflate the total. A station is the location and hosts one or several ports. When Open Charge Map gives no number of ports for a station, the ports are counted as the sum of its connectors, which can overstate them slightly. The IEA publishes ports only; stations come from Open Charge Map and, where an official count exists, from the US DOE (83,559 stations, 262,578 ports) and Natural Resources Canada (14,743 locations, 39,654 public ports, March 2026).
  • Open Charge Map is a sample. It is a crowd-sourced registry that also imports operator and government feeds, so its coverage varies a lot: close to complete in the United States, Canada, Norway, Spain, Iceland and New Zealand; partial in Germany, France, the United Kingdom or Italy; nearly empty in China, South Korea and Vietnam. The "Company data coverage" column divides the ports mapped in Open Charge Map by the IEA total; read the brand and speed splits as the make-up of the mapped network, not of the whole country. Private and removed chargers are excluded; ports = the listed number of points, else the sum of connectors.
  • Speed bands come from the power of each connector in Open Charge Map (<50, 50–99, 100–249, 250–349, 350+ kW); ports of a station are spread over its connectors in proportion. Missing power counts as under 50 kW.
  • Tesla Superchargers come from supercharge.info (community database, open sites only); Tesla also appears in Open Charge Map under several operator names, so use the Tesla box for Tesla and ignore the Tesla line of the OCM list when they differ.
  • Public only. Every figure counts public chargers: the IEA's public ports, Open Charge Map without private or restricted sites, the US DOE and Canadian public counts. Home chargers and private workplace or fleet chargers are not included anywhere.
  • Ratios. Ports per 100,000 people aged 15–64 use the working-age population of this site's Population & Energy page (latest year, applied to every year). Ports per 1,000 EVs divide the IEA ports by the IEA EV stock (battery-electric + plug-in hybrid cars, 2024 and 2025 only).
  • China: the IEA counts 4.7 million public ports (more than the rest of the world together) but Open Charge Map lists almost none. The brand card therefore uses the China Charging Alliance (EVCIPA) ranking of public charging piles by operator for July 2025 (top 15 = 84.1% of public piles), without station or speed split; the Tesla box is available.
